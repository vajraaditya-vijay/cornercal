Installing (a whole five steps, buckle up)

  1. Open the downloaded .dmg and drag CornerCal into Applications. There's a shortcut to the Applications folder right there. We even did the hard part for you.
  2. Open CornerCal once. macOS will slam the door in its face and claim it "can't verify the developer." Correct, and expected — see below for who to blame.
  3. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to CornerCal. This button has main character energy for about an hour after step 2, then it vanishes. Don't dawdle.
  4. Enter your password and click Open in the confirmation dialog.
  5. Allow Calendar access when CornerCal asks. This is the entire reason it exists — refusing here is like buying a car and declining the wheels.

Here's the honest version: CornerCal isn't notarized, because notarizing costs $99 a year for an Apple Developer account, and this is a free app made by a 14-year-old, not a subsidiary of Apple. So macOS treats it like a stranger. The steps above are the official, Apple-sanctioned way to tell your own Mac "I know this app, let it in" — not a hack, not a workaround, just the price of not paying the toll. Prefer Terminal? Skip the clicking entirely: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/CornerCal.app

FAQ

  1. Is this safe? macOS just yelled at me. Yes. The yelling is Apple's Developer Program fee, not a virus scan. CornerCal is one Swift app talking to one system calendar — there's nowhere for anything shady to hide.
  2. Why isn't it on the App Store? Also gated behind $99/year, plus a review process built for companies, not students with a laptop. This site is the whole distribution strategy.
  3. Will it email me, track me, or sell my calendar to advertisers? It would need the internet for any of that. It doesn't use the internet. It doesn't even know the internet exists.
  4. Does it work with Google Calendar / iCloud / my school's Exchange account? If you've already added it in the macOS Calendar app, yes. CornerCal just reads what's already there and lets you pick which calendars show.

Privacy

CornerCal reads events from the macOS Calendar app on your own Mac — whatever accounts you've already connected in System Settings. No account of its own, no server, no analytics, no "anonymous usage data." Everything it shows is computed locally, on your machine, purely so you can stop lying to yourself about being on top of your schedule.
